Hand Evaluation — Two Systems

LTC — Losing Trick Count (suit contracts only)
  • Count losers in each suit — top 3 cards only (max 3 per suit)
  • A = 0 losers · K (with A) = 0 · K (without A) = 1 · Q (without A or K) = 1 · small card = 1 loser per card, up to 3
  • Singleton (not A) = 1 loser · Void = 0 losers · Ace singleton = 0 losers
  • Formula once fit confirmed: 24 − (opener losers + responder losers) = tricks → determine level
  • 9-card fit: subtract 1 from combined losers · 10-card fit: subtract 2
  • Use LTC only after an 8-card trump fit is confirmed
  • LTC does NOT apply to NT contracts — use Banzai for those
  • CRITICAL: Use LTC ONLY after an 8-card suit fit is confirmed. Before fit is found, use HCP/Bergen/Banzai.

Bergen Method — Opening Hand Evaluation
  • 1. Base HCP: A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1
  • 2. Honor Adjustment: Count (#Aces + #Tens) minus (#Queens + #Jacks). If >+2 → add 1 HCP; >+5 → add 2. If <−2 → subtract 1; <−5 → subtract 2
  • 3. Dubious Honors: Subtract 1 for isolated K, Q, J, Qx, Jx, KQ, AJ, KJ, QJ in short suits
  • 4. Length Points: +1 per 5-card suit, +2 per 6-card, +3 per 7-card
  • 5. Suit Quality: +1 if 3 of top 5 honors present in any suit
  • 6. Flatness: −1 for 4333 shape (no ruffing values)
  • After 8-card fit — Support Points: Doubleton +1 · Singleton +2 (+3 with extra trump) · Void = #trumps in hand
  • Extra trump above 8-card fit: +1 per extra trump for both partners
Principle: Honors in long suits >> short suits. Honors in partner's suit = very valuable. Honors in opponent-bid suits = mostly wasted. Only add shortness points after 8-card fit confirmed — never before, never in NT.

19–20 HCP with a 6-Card Suit

Open at 1-level in the 6-card suit, then rebid 3NT on the second round. Partner passes (if balanced and no fit) or picks the suit for game or slam based on LTC/Banzai.

  • 2NT requires balanced hand (no void, at most one doubleton)

Jacoby 2NT Response (Slam Invite)

After partner opens 1-level suit (with or without interference), 2NT by responder = 8-card fit + ≤6 losers (slam interest)
  • Shows confirmed 8-card fit (3+ card support) AND a 6-loser hand — slam-invitational
  • This is artificial and alertable; different from a natural 2NT bid
  • Opener's responses: sign off directly in game (minimum, no slam interest) OR accept the slam invite and use Kickback / cue bids to investigate slam
  • ON even after opponent interference — partner still bids 2NT to show the fit and slam values
  • After Jacoby 2NT is accepted: agree trump suit, then use Kickback for key cards
Example: 1♠–(2♥)–2NT = 3+ spades, ≤6 losers, slam interest. Opener with 6+ losers bids 4♠ (sign off). With 5 or below losers, cuebid or use Kickback.

Overcalls, Doubles & Competitive Bidding

Core philosophy: Suit quality (SQ) matters more than HCP. Get in early, get out quickly. Always ask: do I want this suit led? If yes → overcall. If not → double or pass.

Simple Overcalls

Requirements
  • 1-level: SQ ≥ 7; ≥7 HCP; ≤9.5 losers (NV) or ≤8.5 (V)
  • 2-level: SQ ≥ 8; ≥10 HCP; opening-hand strength
  • 3-level: 15+ HCP (opening-bid plus values) with SQ ≥ 9 — provided partner has NOT passed before
  • 1NT overcall: 15–18 HCP; ≥2 stoppers in opponents' suit
  • SQ = honors + suit length (J or T only count if A/K/Q also present)
  • Rule of 7: assume partner has 7 average points — can you make your bid?
Lighter Bids After Partner Passes
  • All overcalls and competitive bids after partner has passed can be lighter by about a king (roughly 3 HCP)
  • Partner has passed = partner cannot have game-going values; we are purely competing or sacrificing
  • 3-level overcall after partner passes: can do with SQ ≥ 9 and opening hand minimum (12+ HCP) rather than 15+
  • 5332 shape → prefer double over 2-level overcall even when lighter
  • Double THEN overcall = 15+ HCP (regardless of partner's pass)

Doubling Techniques

Takeout Double
  • Always takeout when it is your first bid in the auction
  • Min: 12+ HCP in direct seat (opening hand)
  • Ideal: shortage in opponents' suit (0–2 cards); 3–4 in all unbid suits
  • Mel Rule: 0 cards = 10 HCP; 1 card = 11; 2 = 12; 3 = 13; 4+ = don't double!
Responding to Takeout Double:
  • 0–8 HCP: bid longest suit (must bid even with 0 points)
  • 9–11 HCP: jump bid in longest suit
  • 12+: cue bid opponents' suit or leap to game
  • Balanced with stopper: 1NT (6–10), 2NT (11–12), 3NT (13–16)
  • Always prefer a major over a minor!

Negative Double

After partner opens at 1-level and opponent overcalls a suit (not NT)
  • Double shows 4+ cards in unbid major(s) — suits you cannot bid naturally at the 1-level
  • Also shows 8 or below losers (opening-hand quality or better)
  • NV: 6+ HCP minimum; V: 10+ HCP (add ~5 HCP per extra level of overcall)
  • Double of 1NT overcall = always PENALTY (not negative)
  • If overcall is at 2-level: double shows 4+ in each unbid suit; typically both minors if over 2♥/2♠, or both majors if over 2♣/2♦
  • After negative double, opener rebids: shows 3-card support for responder's implied major (Support Double) or bids naturally

Support, Responsive & Other Doubles

Support Double
  • Opener doubles after responder bids a major + opponent interferes = exactly 3-card support for responder's major
  • With 4-card support: raise directly (not a double)
  • Very important — allows responder to judge 7 vs 8 card fits
Responsive & Lead Directing
  • Responsive: LHO opens, partner doubles, RHO raises → your double = 4-4 in two unbid suits (not the bid suits)
  • Double of artificial bid = lead directing (Stayman, Jacoby, Blackwood, cue bids) — tells partner: lead that suit
  • Negative inference: NOT doubling an artificial bid = that suit is a poor lead choice

  • Bid own 5+ suit = natural overcall
  • If contract is above 2♠ → not mandatory; exercise judgement
All passout seat bids are a king (≈3 HCP) lighter than normal direct seat bids — partner was forced to pass and likely holds some values. Standard requirements throughout reduced by ~3 HCP: a 12 HCP opening becomes a 9 HCP equivalent, a 15 HCP reverse becomes a 12 HCP bid, etc.

Penalty Doubles — Klinger Rules

Rule of 10 (Trump Strength)
  • Your trump tricks + tricks they try ≥ 10
  • 1-level: need 3 trump winners
  • 2-level: need 2 trump winners
  • 3-level: need 1 trump winner
Rule of 12 (Trump Length)
  • #trumps + tricks they try ≥ 12
  • 1-level: 5 trumps needed
  • 2-level: 4 trumps needed
  • 3-level: 3 trumps needed
  • Also: 20+ HCP combined and misfit with partner
NEVER double: (a) 4-level suit contracts unless preempt/sacrifice involved; (b) suit slams — they know they're making; (c) confidently bid game contracts on points alone — need trump suit analysis.

Preemptive Bidding

Preempt when HCP ≤ 8 (or 10–11 HCP with very distributional unbalanced hand). Once you preempt, do NOT bid again unless partner makes an invitational or forcing bid.

Slam Bidding

Never bid close slams. Only bid when you've confirmed a very good chance. +450 making 5♥ beats −50 going down in 6♥ at most tables. IMP teams: bid slams only if >75% probability.

Bid the suit immediately ABOVE the agreed trump at the 4-level = asks for key cards (4 Aces + King of trumps).

Gerber Convention (NT Slams Only)

4♣ after a NT bid = Gerber (ace-asking)
Ace responses:
  • 4♦ = 0 or 4 Aces
  • 4♥ = 1 Ace
  • 4♠ = 2 Aces
  • 4NT = 3 Aces
  • Then 5♣ = ask for Kings (same steps: 5♦=0/4K, 5♥=1K, 5♠=2K, 5NT=3K)
  • Use Gerber ONLY after a NT bid (1NT open, 2NT open, 1NT/2NT rebid, 3NT bid)
  • Do NOT use 4♣ Gerber in a suit auction — that would be a natural club bid or cue bid
  • 6NT rule: Bid 6NT if you hold at least 7 of the 8 top honours (4 Aces + 4 Kings) combined. With only 6 of 8 → settle for 5NT. Missing 2+ Aces → never bid 6NT.
  • NT slam Banzai: 49+ combined for 6NT; 56+ for 7NT
  • DEPO if opponents overcall 4♣: Double = Even aces; Pass = Odd

Control / Cue Bid Slam Convention

  • Use when: weak holding in any suit (xx, Qxx, Jx) or void — need to identify specific controls before key-card ask
  • Cue bids start above 3-level of agreed major, or above 3NT if agreed minor is trump
  • Italian method: show first OR second round control, up-the-line in order
  • First round = Ace or Void · Second round = King or Singleton
  • Skip a suit = no control in that suit → partner signs off in trump suit
  • Sequence: Fit confirmed → Cue bids up the line → Kickback → Queen ask → King ask → Final contract
  • OFF if opponents interfere

Jacoby 2NT & Splinters

Modified Jacoby 2NT
  • After partner opens any suit: 2NT = slam interest + 3+ card fit
  • Opener: sign off in game, or accept and use Kickback
  • Alertable; ON even with opponent interference
Splinter Bids
  • Double jump response = singleton or void + game-going values
  • Examples: 1♦-3♣; 1♥-3♦/3♣; 1♠-3♣/3♦/3♥
  • Partner recalculates LTC with singleton/void upgrade and decides on game or slam
  • Best response to splinter: A/x/x or three small in splinter suit

Signs NOT to Bid Slam

No control in unbid suit (2 quick losers) · Misfits / wasted values opposite partner's shortness · No ruffing values · Both balanced hands · Too many Q/J with few A/K · 3+ losers in a single suit · Missing more than 1 key card

Loser Elimination — Priority Order

1st: Pitch (~100%)
  • Discard loser on established winner in short hand
  • Need to set up winners first (often defer trumps)
  • Risk only: pitch ruffed if trumps not yet drawn
2nd: Ruff in Short Hand
  • Trump loser in the SHORT hand (dummy)
  • 2nd round ruff: safe. 4th round: expect overruff
  • Ruffing in master hand = no extra tricks
3rd: Finesse (50%)
  • Last resort only
  • Always lead toward the honor — never from it
  • Check for endplay before committing to finesse

When NOT to Draw Trumps Immediately

  • Need to ruff in short hand — can't ruff after trumps are exhausted
  • Immediate avoidable side-suit losers — must eliminate before losing the lead
  • Entry issues — an essential entry consumed by drawing trumps
  • Only boss trump outstanding — often safe to leave it out
  • Bad trump split discovered — may need to ruff both ways or exploit tenace
  • Draw trumps immediately only about 40% of time; defer in 60% of cases
Ducking to draw trumps faster: When missing 3 of 5 trump honors, duck the first round (give away the trick early) — you then draw remaining trumps fast without losing the lead repeatedly.

NT Contract Planning

  1. Count sure winners in each suit using Banzai perspective
  2. Identify blockages — unblock them first
  3. Choose which suit to establish (length, entries, timing, quality)
  4. Decide where to win trick 1 (hold-up? win immediately?)
  5. Lose your losers EARLY (duck) to preserve entries to the long suit
Always lead toward your honor — never play the honor from your hand first. KQJ2 vs AT98: play small from hand toward KQJ. Playing the honor first gives away tricks unnecessarily.
Ducking rule: Only path to contract requires establishing a long suit? Duck 1–2 tricks early. With AK in a 5-2 suit needing 3-3 break → duck once. With A only → duck twice. If suit breaks 4-2 instead → duck again when you discover the bad break.

Counting & Card Reading

  • Start counting from the auction — every bid tells you shape and strength
  • Use discovery plays before the critical suit: play suits with known winners first
  • Once an opponent shows out → complete shape of all 4 hands is known
  • If a preemptor leads a different suit mid-hand → almost certainly a singleton
  • Locating the Q: more cards a hand has in a suit → Q more likely there
  • NT opening lead gives suit-length information about the leader's hand
  • In slam contracts: do discovery plays before touching critical finesse suits; don't rush

Defence & Signalling System

Defensive Strategy

Passive Defence (Default)
  • Default unless evidence demands active — "first do no harm"
  • Safe leads: solid sequences (KQJ), honor-less long suits, opponents' strong suits
  • Do NOT lead from A, K, or Q without a compelling reason
  • When to use: opponents limp into game; partner is weak; against 1NT or 6NT
Active Defence
  • Dummy has long running suit → go active IMMEDIATELY
  • Knock out side suit entries to dummy's long suit — if dummy has a long running suit but needs entries to cash it, attack those entries (the aces/kings that let declarer reach dummy) before they are used
  • Opponents took a slam try and stopped in game → they have extra values → attack
  • Against small slams → be active early; every tempo matters
  • Against preempt contracts → cash your tricks quickly before they vanish
  • Passive leads into active situations give declarer free tricks

Opening Leads

Lead Styles

Smith Echo (NT Contracts)

After partner's opening lead in NT, when declarer gains the lead and plays a suit: HIGH = encourage partner's opening lead suit (continue when possible); LOW = discouraging (consider switching). Powerful coordination tool for NT defence.

Trump Defence Strategies

Lead Trumps When...
  • Auction was a preference (declarer bid 2 suits, dummy chose one)
  • Dummy expected to be short in declarer's side suit
  • You have strength in declarer's second suit
  • Partner made a penalty double → ALWAYS lead trumps
Forcing Defence
  • Use when YOU have 4+ trumps
  • Lead your long suit → force declarer to ruff in his hand
  • Repeat forcing leads → declarer eventually has fewer trumps than you
  • You gain trump control → take tricks that would otherwise go to ruffing

Matchpoints — Critical Principles

  • Field contract is everything: Target tricks based on what other tables will be playing
  • Underbid contract: Play safe — hope others go down in game
  • Overbid contract: Take all risks — finesse, assume good breaks; already in trouble

  • Plus score is king: Make your contract; never risk it for overtricks

BAM (Board-a-Match)

Coward's game. Sit back, let opponents err. Don't make close penalty doubles, sacrifice more freely, do NOT overbid. Every board = simple win or loss vs just one other table.

🎯 Bidding Tips & Principles
Golden Rules of Bidding
Bridge is 80% about bidding correctly to the most optimum contract. We win on card play only 20%.
Whenever game is confirmed, jump directly there with a jump bid — avoid giving bidding space to opponents.
Never leave opponents to play in an 8-card fit at the 2-level (Law of Total Tricks).
Always visualise the final contract BEFORE making your first bid. Plan a second bid before choosing a first.
With 2 suits of equal length, open the higher-ranking suit — partner can then leave you or put you back at a lower level.
Always strive to make the stronger hand (higher HCP) the declarer. Opponents lead away from their strength.
Hand Evaluation Upgrades & Downgrades
Upgrade: A & K (good), concentrated honors, honors in long suits, intermediates (T,9,8) in long suits, 6-card suit or singleton.
Downgrade: Q & J alone (bad), scattered honors, honors in short suits, flat 4333 shape (subtract 1 HCP).
If LHO bid a suit and you have AQ or Kx in it → downgrade. If RHO bid it → upgrade.
Honors in partner's long suit are far more valuable than honors in opponent-bid suits.
A 9-card fit is worth 2 extra HCP over an 8-card fit. A 10-card fit is worth 5 more.
Points in long suits are worth far more than points in short suits.
Competitive Auction Decisions
3-over-2 Rule: Never let opponents play comfortably at 2-level when they have found an 8-card fit — always compete from the passout seat.
3-over-3 Rule: Only with 9-card fit + shortage in opponent suit OR 8-card fit + excellent 5-card side suit.
4-over-3 Rule: Never bid part score at 4-level yourself. Leave opponents at 3-level.
If opponents have a fit, we almost certainly have one too. Compete accordingly.
If opponents haven't found a fit yet, be cautious about competing — partner is less likely to hold a fit too.
Do not allow opponents to play below 2♠ when NV. Manufacture a bid in the passout seat.
Most Common Bidding Mistakes
Adding support points (shortness) before an 8-card fit is confirmed — never do this early.
Responding 1NT instead of a suit when you have a 4-card major to show — always show the major first.
Not supporting partner's overcall with 3+ trumps.
Overcalling with a 4-card suit especially in IMPs — always need 5+ cards.
Not doubling when opponents are sacrificing against you (especially when V).
Raising your own preempt — you surrendered captaincy when you preempted. Pass!
Law of Total Tricks (LoTT) — Shortcut Guide
LoTT formula: Total Tricks = sum of longest trump fit for each side. Bid to the level = your trump count.
Shortcut: Sum of both sides' trump fits minus 11 = safe level. E.g. 8+8=16, 16−11=5 → 2-level safe.
16 total trumps: Do NOT bid 3 over 3. 17: Bid 3 over 3 only when NV. 18+: Always bid 3 over 3.
With 8-card fit → safe at 2-level. 9-card fit → safe at 3-level. 10-card → safe at 4-level.
Adjust LoTT DOWN: Misfits, flat 4333 hands, minor honors (Jxx, Qx) in opponent suits, no intermediates.
Adjust LoTT UP: Double fits, 5-5 distribution, voids, strong intermediates in own suit, pure hands.
The 5-level belongs to the opponents — only bid there with 20+ total trumps or freak distribution.
When in doubt: bid one more when NV. Bidding is easier than defending in bridge!
🃏 Declarer Play Tips
Suit Contracts — Core Principles
Priority order: 1. Pitch losers on established winners (100%) → 2. Ruff in short hand → 3. Finesse (50%).
Draw trumps immediately only ~40% of the time. In 60% of cases there is a good reason to defer.
When you need to ruff in dummy, almost NEVER draw trumps first.
When missing 3 of 5 trump honors — consider ducking first round to draw remaining trumps quickly.
Whenever you see a 5-2 or 6-1 side suit + solid trumps → first thought must be to establish that long suit.
Bad trumps is NOT a reason to avoid drawing trumps. Draw them to limit opponents' ruffing power.
NT Contracts — Core Principles
Order: 1. Establish long suit → 2. Finesse → 3. Hold-up / Ducking.
First check for blockages in any suit — unblock BEFORE making any other plan.
9 out of 10 NT contracts are a race to drive out honors and win with intermediates and long cards.
Attack the suit with the highest GUARANTEED extra tricks first — not always the longest suit.
Exception: If attacking the best suit gives opponents the lead and they defeat you, choose a suit with potential tricks (even a finesse) to at least give you a chance.
In NT, lose your losers early (duck) to preserve entries. With AK in 5-2 suit needing 3-3 break: duck once. Only A: duck twice.
When to Choose NT vs Suit Contract
Prefer NT: Extra values (29+ HCP); flat distribution (4333); 5-3 or 6-2 fits; when opponents preempt (hold up shuts them out).
Prefer suit: 4-4 fit (always play the major); when you'll ruff in the short hand; partner has preempted; long weak hand has no re-entry.
At slam level: if 12 tricks are secure and biggest risk is a ruff → choose 6NT (safer — can't be ruffed).
4-4 fit gives 1 extra trick over NT (ruff in either hand). 5-3 fit gives same tricks as NT in most cases.
Advanced Techniques
Deception as declarer: Play lower of equals to make defender duck. Play higher of equals to make defender cover.
False carding: Do this as declarer often. NEVER as defender (partner gets deceived too).
Play as high a card as possible on opening lead (close to East's card) to confuse opener's count/attitude.
Not overruffing: Sometimes discard instead of overruffing — you might score 2 tricks instead of 1.
If a contract seems doomed to finesse incorrectly, try playing for the finesse card as a singleton — last resort!
Endplay: When a contract depends only on a finesse, look for an endplay — strip side suits + trumps, then put the right defender on lead.
Counting & Card Reading
Start counting from the AUCTION. Every bid tells you shape and strength before the first card is played.
Use discovery plays before the critical suit. Play suits with known outcomes first to locate the key card.
Once any opponent shows out → the complete shape of all 4 hands is known. Use it fully!
If a preemptor leads a different suit mid-hand → almost certainly a singleton.
In NT: always use the Rule of 11 (card led = 4th highest; 11 minus its face value = cards higher than it in the other 3 hands).
Useful exercise: when dummy appears, predict declarer's EXACT shape. Check at the end. This single habit dramatically improves defensive card reading.
Common Declarer Mistakes
Drawing trumps when you need to ruff something in dummy first.
Playing the honor from your hand first (KQJ2) instead of leading toward it from the opposite hand.
Taking the first trick with a stopper when you should hold up to cut communication.
Not counting losers before playing to trick 1 (KLEPTO — K is for Knowledge first).
Trying to make overtricks in IMPs — risking a making contract for 1 extra IMP is never worth it.
In MP: playing safe when you're in a contract the field doesn't bid — you need to maximize tricks.
🛡️ Defence Tips & Signals
Active vs Passive Defence
Default = Passive — "First, do no harm." Don't lead away from honors unless you have a specific reason.
Go ACTIVE when: dummy has a long running suit; opponents stopped in game after slam try; against small slams.
The moment you see a long running suit in dummy → attack immediately, even leading from a king.
Against preempt contracts → cash your tricks quickly before they disappear.
When partner is weak → don't lead a singleton hoping for a ruff. Partner may never get in to give you one. Lead your longest suit instead.
Signalling Priorities
When partner leads → show Attitude first (do I like/dislike this suit?).
When declarer leads → show Count (how many cards do I hold?).
When neither Attitude nor Count is useful (dummy has singleton, or giving partner a ruff) → Suit Preference.
First discard always = Lavinthal (suit preference: high = want that suit; low = don't want it).
Smith Echo: In NT, when declarer plays a suit after gaining the lead — High = like partner's opening lead. Low = switch.
Double of artificial bid = lead directing. Not doubling = negative inference (don't lead that suit).
Common Defence Mistakes
Playing 3rd hand low — always play high in 3rd position (except returning partner's suit).
Not leading partner's bid suit — respect partner's information from the auction.
Not returning partner's suit when you win a trick.
Not covering an honor with an honor — cover always (forces declarer to use 2 honors for 1 trick).
Leading an unsupported Ace — you may establish declarer's K or Q for free.
Just cashing all your high cards without a plan — declarer may discard losers on your second winners.
Opening Lead Principles
Choose lead based on auction: Finesse = passive; Trump defence sequence = lead trump; Dump/establish = active.
In NT: make "normal" opening leads. Don't try for a top by choosing an unusual lead — it usually backfires.
Against suit small slam → be active. Your tricks may disappear quickly.
Leading low = you have an honor in that suit. Leading high = honor-less suit (top of nothing).
If bidding tells you exactly where declarer's strength lies → lead the weakest-looking suit to avoid giving a trick.
If you had overcalled a suit in the bidding, partner should lead it unless signalled otherwise.
🏆 Tournament Tactics — Doubles Dummies Checklist
Matchpoints (MP) — Key Tactics
Field contract determines your play — know what others will be in and plan accordingly.
Overbid contract: take all risks (assume good breaks, finesse freely). Underbid: play safe.
Uranium Rule: Double vulnerable part scores aggressively — +200 (V, down 1 doubled) = near top.
Prefer 4M over 3NT with a 4-4 major fit (exception: 29+ combined HCP → 3NT).
Balance freely NV — do not let opponents sit comfortably at 1NT or 2-level.
10-point NT bonus matters! Consider 4NT over 4M if sure of tricks.
IMPs / Teams — Key Tactics
Plus score is everything — never risk your contract for an overtrick (1 IMP gain vs 6-10 IMP loss).
Stretch to bid games: V = bid 40%+ games; NV = 50%+ games.
Never double part scores — +300 saves only 4 IMPs; going wrong costs 7+.
If it's hard for you, it's hard for them too at the other table. Don't panic into wild bids.
As defender: take risks with opening lead if it could set the contract — the reward outweighs a minor cost.
Safety plays are mandatory even at cost of an overtrick.
Penalty Doubles & Sacrifices
MP: Double part scores liberally at 3-level and above. "Magic 200" — V opponent down 1 doubled = near top.
IMPs: Don't double unless you expect the contract to go 2 down. Down 1 doubled gives you little extra; doubling game that makes is catastrophic.
Penalty double a suit game only based on how trump suit splits — NOT based on points alone.
Sacrifice when NV vs V opponents — going down 2 (-300) beats their game (-400 or more).
Never sacrifice with a flat (4333 or 4432) hand — no ruffing values = losers stay losers.
With void in opponents' trump suit + strong hand → stripe-tailed ape double is an option (sacrifice strategy).
🔬 Advanced Plays & Concepts
Squeeze Play
A squeeze opportunity exists when: the only path to making the contract is a 3-3 suit split AND you have a card in another suit that becomes a winner if a defender discards a guard card.
To induce a squeeze: play all your established winners — even the last certain trump. Opponents are forced to discard something useful.
In NT: if anyways you're going to lose a trick, play that suit first to get extra distribution information — use it for the critical finesse later.
Count cards carefully during the entire hand. A squeeze only works if you track what each defender is guarding.
Hold-up Play & Ducking
Hold-up: Withhold a winner deliberately to cut communication between defenders. Powerful in NT and even suit contracts.
Ducking: Lose a trick deliberately to establish an entry to the long suit. Different from hold-up (which is about communication).
Benefits of hold-up in suit contracts: breaks defender communication, forces their choice of defense, you control when to ruff.
In IMPs: duck a trick early to protect against 4-1 suit breaks. One overtrick isn't worth a blown contract.
Principle of Fast Arrival
Direct jump to game in a GF auction = minimum GF range, no slam interest, pre-emptive effect on opponents.
Slow approach through low bids in a GF auction = extra values, slam interest. Give partner space to evaluate.
Fast arrival does NOT apply to 3NT — there is no "Fast Arrival 3NT." Jumps to 3NT in GF auctions actually show EXTRA values (15–17 HCP range).
Example: 1♠-4♠ = game values, no slam. 1♠-2♦-2♥-2♠-4♠ = extra values, just not enough for slam.
